Do you remember a time you were sure you were doing the right thing, and it turned out you made things worse? You meant well. You tried hard. And still, something broke.

Most of us carry a quiet belief: *if my heart is in the right place, things will work out.* It sounds humble. It is actually the opposite.

## Good intentions are not a compass

Intentions tell you where you *want* to go. They do not tell you where the road actually leads. A driver who means to go north but trusts a broken compass will still end up south, however sincere they are.

That is what Proverbs is warning about. Not that your understanding is worthless, but that it is **not enough on its own**. It was never designed to carry the full weight of your life.

## The cost you do not see

Here is the hard part. When you lean only on your own understanding, the damage is often invisible:

- An opportunity slips by and you never know it was there.
- A friend slowly drifts away and you cannot explain why.
- A pattern repeats in every relationship and it always seems to be someone else's fault.

You can live a whole lifetime not doing the right thing without ever knowing why. That is the real danger. Not dramatic failure, but a slow, quiet miss.

## What leaning on God looks like on a Tuesday

"Acknowledge him in all thy ways" is not only about Sunday. It is practical.

### Ask before you act

Before a big decision, pause and ask: *what does scripture say about this?* Not to find a verse that agrees with you, but to be open to one that doesn't.

### Invite correction

Wisdom rarely arrives alone. Let a mentor, a home group or a trusted friend speak into your plans. Pride says "I've got this." Wisdom says "what am I missing?"

### Keep learning on purpose

Knowledge of God's word does not arrive by accident. Five minutes a day, done faithfully, will change the way you see more than an occasional marathon ever will.

## Good is where it starts

Being a good person is a beginning, not a destination. The invitation is bigger: to trade *my* understanding for *his* direction, one ordinary decision at a time.

**Today's step:** think of one decision you are facing this week. Before you make it, read Proverbs 3 slowly and ask where you might be leaning on yourself alone.
